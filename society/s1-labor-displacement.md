# S1 — AI and White-Collar Labor Displacement

*Last Updated: November 27, 2026*
*Track 1: Economics & Labor · Companion: `../md/28-capabilities-2030-future-interfaces.md`*

**The one-line finding:** the entry-level disruption is real and measured in three independent datasets; the aggregate unemployment effect is still ~zero; and the 2030 question is whether the "missing ladder" becomes mid-career scarcity or mid-career displacement — the evidence cannot yet distinguish these.

---

## 1. The Occupation Scoreboard (evidence through Nov 2026)

| Occupation | Verdict | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Translation** | DISPLACED | BLS projects **-12%** (2024–34), explicitly citing gen-AI; postings -35% YoY (UK/DE); IMF translators ~200→50; CEPR: ~28k US jobs never created |
| **Customer support** | DISPLACED | CSR jobs **-130k (-4.8%)** in year to May 2025 (BLS); Salesforce support 9,000→5,000 (on record); Forrester: 49% of CS jobs gone by 2030 |
| **Software — junior** | DISPLACED | New-grad hiring **-65%** at big tech, **-76%** at early-stage startups vs 2019 (SignalFire); new CS-grad unemployment ~6–7%, now above art history; Salesforce hired zero new engineers FY2026 |
| **Software — senior** | AUGMENTED | Big-tech senior eng hiring only -11%; early-stage startups +7% engineers in 2025; BLS still projects +15% developer growth. The split is cohort-based, not occupational |
| **Copy/content** | DISPLACED (commodity tier) | Upwork writing postings -32% YoY; content share of posts -31% (OpenAI–Upwork); but AI-skilled freelancers earn ~40% more |
| **Data entry/admin** | DISPLACED | McKinsey: ~7.5M US roles projected to disappear; Atlanta Fed CFO survey: -2.19% routine-clerical share by 2028 |
| **Bookkeeping** | PRESSURED→DISPLACED at bottom | BLS -6%; clerk tier shrinks, AI-augmented reviewer tier gains — classic hollowing |
| **Paralegals** | PRESSURED | BLS growth cut to +1%; ~69% of billable work automatable; AI-skilled paralegals earn 15–25% premiums |
| **Lawyers** | AUGMENTED | No employment decline; liability + bar rules keep humans in the loop |
| **Finance analysts** | PRESSURED (early) | Top-exposed in Anthropic Economic Index API traffic; weakest-evidence category — exposure scores, not outcomes yet |
| **Recruiting** | PRESSURED | Headcount -14% vs 2021 while applications +93%; screening automated, judgment/closing retained |
| **Marketing managers** | AUGMENTED | Manager postings +20% while execution roles beneath fell — the management layer absorbs AI leverage |
| **Care/trades** (contrast) | UNAFFECTED | 2026 hiring rebound concentrated in healthcare + skilled trades; forecasters expect care/service share to *rise* to 48% by 2030 |

## 2. The Macro Picture

**What shows up in the data:**
- **BLS has formally flagged 18 "AI-related" occupations** that declined -0.2% while total employment grew +0.8% — real divergence, tiny aggregate.
- **The white-collar stall is real:** finance + information + professional services employment peaked **April 2023** and is -2% since, while all other sectors are +3.7%. "Jobless growth" is confined to the professional class.
- **The entry-level divergence is the single most robust finding.** Stanford's "Canaries" study (ADP payroll microdata): 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations are **19% below peers** (Aug 2026 update) — and the decline appears in *automation*-type AI usage but not *augmentation*-type. It survived robustness checks (dropping tech entirely, controlling for remote work).
- **Anthropic Economic Index** (latest: "Cadences," Jun 2026): claude.ai chats are 52% augmentation / 45% automation — but **enterprise API traffic is ~75–77% automation**, and that's the channel that maps to job redesign. Adoption is diffusing ~10x faster than historical technologies.
- **AI-attributed layoffs 2025–26:** Amazon ~30k across two rounds ("we will need fewer people doing some of the jobs being done today"), IBM >15k cumulative, Salesforce support, ~20 others on TechCrunch's running list.

**What doesn't show up:**
- No aggregate effect: unemployment 4.3%, claims historically low. Acemoglu's small-net-negative estimate stands unrefuted.
- Adoption is shallow: only ~12% of large US firms had AI in production by Q3 2025 (EIG) — economy-wide displacement arithmetic doesn't work yet.
- Attribution is contested: exposed-occupation postings began falling in **2022, pre-ChatGPT**; rates and remote work are rival explanations; EIG's sharpest finding is that *adopting firms aren't the ones driving the decline*.

## 3. "This Time Is Different" — Both Sides Steelmanned

**It IS different:**
1. **The capability curve has no precedent** — METR horizon doubling every ~3.6 months; Remote Labor Index 2.5% → 41.3% in 13 months; GDPval near 50% expert parity at ~100x speed/cost. No prior general-purpose technology improved on a months-scale doubling.
2. **The canary pattern matches the mechanism** — declines concentrate exactly where theory predicts: young workers, automation-type usage, codified tasks.
3. **This wave targets the cognitive middle class** (45% of employment) and removes the training ladder first, which compounds over a decade.

**It is NOT different (yet):**
1. **Timing doesn't fit** — declines pre-date ChatGPT; 12% production adoption can't explain macro claims; rates + overhiring corrections explain most tech layoffs.
2. **Complementarity dominates measured deployments** — call-center RCTs show +15% productivity with the largest gains to the *least* skilled.
3. **Jevons:** token prices fell ~48x while consumption rose 100–1000x; comparative advantage preserves paid human work even under absolute AI superiority (Noah Smith). Counter-counter: demand for *software* explodes; demand for *software engineers as we knew them* may not.
4. **The prophets blinked.** Amodei (May 2025: "50% of entry-level white-collar jobs in 1–5 years") by mid-2026: *"I thought there would have been more impact… than has actually happened."* Altman softened similarly — both while eyeing IPOs. Note the incentive contamination cuts both ways: doom rhetoric sold capability; soft rhetoric sells IPOs.
5. **Klarna is the template:** 700 cut → quality crisis → public reversal → settled state that is *still* AI-first (80% of chats) with far fewer humans. First-wave cuts overshoot; the post-reversal equilibrium still has fewer people.

## 4. Projections to 2028/2030

**Anchor forecasts:** NBER/FRI expert survey — white-collar job growth just +2% 2025–30 vs 6.8% trend; 1 in 4 experts expect net losses >4%; unemployment 5–6% by 2030 in *all* scenarios. Goldman (Jun 2026) — 25% of work hours automatable; 6–7% of jobs displaced over the adoption period, 72% of them white-collar. McKinsey (Oct 2026) — ~11M US workers (≈7%) must switch occupations by 2035; ~27% of work hours automated by 2030.

| Scenario | ~Prob. | 2028 | 2030 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Augmented hollowing** (base) | ~55% | Entry-level hiring stays 40–65% below 2019 in exposed fields; CSR/translation/data-entry down another 10–20%; junior wage compression, AI-fluent senior premiums | White-collar share flat to -1pt; 6–7% cumulative displacement underway; a visible "missing cohort" of 2024–28 graduates; no unemployment crisis |
| **Acceleration** (capability curves hold + reliability threshold crossed) | ~25% | Mid-tier roles (analysts, accountants, L2 support) begin absolute declines; white-collar recession visible in payrolls | Amodei's original 10–20% entry-level claim lands 2–4 years late; unemployment 6%+. Weakest link: the benchmark→labor mapping |
| **Plateau/absorption** | ~20% | Reliability/liability walls; Klarna-style reversals generalize; Jevons demand dominates | Task recomposition, ~zero net employment effect; Acemoglu vindicated |

**Tie-back to doc 28:** the capability table there (week-to-month reliable autonomy by 2028) maps to the *acceleration* scenario's mechanism — agents climbing the seniority curve. But S1's evidence says the binding constraint on displacement is the same one doc 28 identified for interfaces: **verification cost and reliability**, not raw capability. The displacement rate through 2028 will track the 80%-reliability horizon, not the 50% one.

**Highest-confidence call:** entry-level disruption deepens through 2028 under every scenario while aggregate unemployment stays historically normal through at least 2028.

---

## Key Uncertainties

- RLI 41.3% ≠ 41.3% of remote work (bounded deliverables only; denominator critiqued).
- The junior-dev "-65–67% postings" figures circulate through weak aggregators; SignalFire's new-grad numbers are the defensible core.
- Causality for the entry-level collapse remains genuinely contested (AI vs rates vs remote work vs pre-2022 trend).
- No Anthropic Economic Index release since June 2026 — "latest AEI" claims in late-2026 coverage refer to older data.
