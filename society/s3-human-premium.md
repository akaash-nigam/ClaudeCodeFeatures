# S3 — The Human Premium

*Last Updated: November 27, 2026*
*Track 1: Economics & Labor · Companions: `s1-labor-displacement.md`, `s2-token-economy.md`*

**The one-line finding:** the inverse of displacement is real and measurable — the economy increasingly pays for someone to *be responsible* rather than to *do the work*. The best-evidenced appreciating position is domain expertise + AI fluency + willingness to own outcomes; the single best risk-adjusted path in the data is, remarkably, the electrical trades — funded by the AI buildout itself.

## 2. The Three Load-Bearing Findings

**The professionalised/democratised split (PwC, 1B+ postings).** AI *professionalises* 22% of advertised jobs — AI does the routine work, the human does expert work; these grow 2x faster with 39% salary growth. AI *democratises* 52% — AI does the complex part, anyone can do the rest; these stagnate at 17%. Junior roles in exposed occupations are now **7x more likely to require traditionally senior skills** — the quantified version of S1's entry-level collapse.

**The accountability premium is the durable one.** No US medical specialty has shrunk due to AI; insurance regulators are *adding* documented-human-judgment requirements (24–25 states adopted the NAIC bulletin, some presume AI-influenced denials invalid without independent human clinical judgment). The literature's warning label: the "liability sink" — humans inserted as click-to-approve absorb liability without exercising judgment. The trajectory is a **"signatory era"**: the premium shifts from doing the work to owning its consequences, and headcount per signature falls.

**The AI boom pays its own displaced.** Datacenter electrical work is 45–70% of build cost, concentrating a ~500K-worker construction shortfall onto electricians. Gen Z sees it: vocational enrollment +20% since 2020, HVACR programs +29% then +17% in consecutive years, with 65% saying a degree won't protect them from AI. Moravec's paradox moved, it didn't vanish: reasoning got cheap; dexterous manipulation didn't.

## 3. Theory That's Tracking

- **Baumol inverted into a prediction engine:** when cognition is cheap, spending migrates to what can't be sped up — care, education, presence, judgment. (Andreessen invoked Baumol from the Fed's AI task force in Nov 2026.)
- **Comparative advantage (Noah Smith):** "humans will always have a comparative advantage at knowing what we want" — tracking well against the agent-manager posting data.
- **The constitutive-human rule (Imas + arXiv provenance work):** the humanness premium accrues only where human presence is *scarce, verified, and constitutive* of the good. Unverified ordinary human labor gets no premium.

## 4. Durable vs Transitional (2028/2030)

**Durable through 2030:** embodied skilled work tied to the physical buildout (highest confidence); licensed care; sign-off authority (though hollowing into the signatory era); live/constitutive-human experiences (concentrated at the top); and taste/problem-selection — "knowing what to want" is the last comparative advantage standing in every theoretical treatment.

**Transitional — expect compression by 2028–30:**
- The raw AI-fluency premium (classic diffusion premium — 55% of employers already pay nothing extra; what persists is the *combination* premium)
- Routine verification (AI verifiers absorb the checking; the *endorsement* premium survives and merges with sign-off)
- "Agent manager" as a standalone title (follows the prompt-engineer arc: title dies ~2029, skill folds into every manager's job — prompt engineering already ran this experiment: title -30%, skill +250%)
- Honor-system "human-made" labels (only cryptographically attested provenance retains value; "human-made" becomes the new "organic" — real but 5–15% of the market)
- Datacenter trades *super*-premiums (capex-cycle-dependent; an S2-style correction compresses the top band while median trades wages stay elevated)

## 5. Practical Implications

1. **Stack, don't pick:** domain expertise + AI fluency + accountability is the best-evidenced position.
2. **Move toward the signature** — licenses are appreciating options even as the underlying work automates. If partway to one, finish it.
3. **Young and choosing now:** electrical trades (datacenter certs) are the best risk-adjusted path in the data; nursing is the adjacent play.
4. **Avoid democratised seats** — if AI makes your job doable by a non-expert, wage growth is already half your professionalised peers'. Climb to the judgment layer or switch.
5. **Treat agent ops/evals/FDE as 3–5 year accelerators, not destinations.**
6. **Creators: make human presence verifiable and constitutive** — live, certified, relationship-based.
7. **Cultivate the residual:** taste, problem selection, relationships, deciding what's worth doing.

**Honest tensions:** the biggest care job (home health aide, $34,900 median, +681K jobs) has booming demand but *no wage power* — demand growth ≠ premium; the accountability premium risks being laundering rather than judgment; and most evidence is advertised salaries, which lead but don't always match realized wages.

**Tie-back to the series:** S3 prices what doc 28 predicted — the "human-bandwidth registry" (which decisions still require a human) is exactly where the durable premium concentrates; and the verification-stack interfaces of 2028 are where the transitional checking premium gets automated away while the endorsement premium survives.
